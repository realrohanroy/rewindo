# Episode 03 — One Hundred and Eighty-Three

**You are:** Kapil Dev, captain of India
**When:** 25 June 1983, Lord's Cricket Ground, London
**Play time:** 15–20 minutes, 7 decisions
**Category:** Sport
**Status:** first draft. All dialogue is dramatised. Items marked **[verify]** need checking against the scorecard and the sources at the bottom. Most of the people in this story are alive: see section 8 before release.

---

## 1. Design notes

### The idea of the episode

1991 was a clock. 1975 was a mirror. This one is a number.

**183.** It is not enough. Everyone at Lord's knows it is not enough, including the eleven men who made it. The episode is about what a captain does with a total that cannot be defended, against the best team that has ever played the game, with the whole ground waiting for the formalities to end.

The player cannot bowl the ball or take the catch by skill. What they control is what a captain controls: who gets the ball, where the fielders stand, what is said in the dressing room, and whether to trust a man who asks for one more over.

### What the player knows (and doesn't)

Only what Kapil Dev knew at each moment. The script never says "the famous catch" or "the ball that changed everything" before it happens. On most paths, those moments do not happen at all.

### Meters

| Meter | Start | What it means | Lose condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Runs in hand** | set by Scene 1 (183 on the real path) | Your total minus West Indies' score. The hero number. | 0 = West Indies win |
| **Wickets** | 0 of 10 | West Indian wickets taken | 10 = India win |
| **Belief** | 30/100 | Whether your eleven think this can be done | Below 15: catches go down |
| **Their swagger** | 90/100 | How freely West Indies are playing | High swagger bleeds runs faster |

Runs in hand falls at the start of every scene, by an amount that depends on **Their swagger**. Wickets slow the bleed. The player watches the number shrink the way the team did: over by over.

### Flags

`batted` (attacked / anchored) · `talk` (fight / anger / silence) · `newball` (sandhu / binny / madan) · `madan` (trusted / removed) · `catch` (called / left) · `lloyd` (attacked / defended) · `partnership` (amarnath / pace / spin)

### Recurring voices

| Voice | Role | Pushes for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunil Gavaskar** | Opening batsman, former captain, the senior man | Percentages. Protect what you have. |
| **Mohinder "Jimmy" Amarnath** | Vice-captain | Calm. Patience. Let them make the mistake. |
| **Madan Lal** | Medium-pacer | The ball. Now. |
| **Syed Kirmani** | Wicketkeeper | What the batsman is thinking, from one yard behind him |
| **P.R. Man Singh** | Team manager | The occasion, and keeping it small |

All five are real. Their lines are invented for the game and kept to cricket. See section 8.

## 2. Briefing (shown before Scene 1)

> **Lord's, London. Saturday, 25 June 1983.**
>
> You are twenty-four years old. You grew up in Chandigarh, and when you first asked for more food at a training camp because fast bowlers need to eat, an official laughed and told you India does not produce fast bowlers.
>
> In two previous World Cups, India won one match. It was against East Africa.
>
> The bookmakers gave you odds of 66 to 1. **[verify]** One English writer promised to eat his words if you won. Your own board booked the team's flights home for before the semi-finals.
>
> A week ago, at a small ground in Tunbridge Wells, you walked out at 17 for 5 against Zimbabwe and made 175 not out. There was a strike at the BBC that day. No camera recorded it.
>
> Today is the final. Across the corridor are the West Indies: champions in 1975, champions in 1979, never beaten in a World Cup final. Roberts. Marshall. Holding. Garner. Greenidge. Haynes. Lloyd. Richards.
>
> Clive Lloyd has won the toss. He has asked you to bat.

## 3. Scenes

### Scene 1 — Ninety-Two for Four

**Time:** early afternoon · **Place:** the middle · **Score:** India 92 for 4

Gavaskar went in the third over. Srikkanth hit Roberts on one knee through the covers and made the ground gasp, and then he went too. Amarnath is out. Yashpal is out.

You walk down the pavilion steps, through the Long Room, past the members in their ties, and out into the light. The slope runs across the ground from left to right. Joel Garner is six feet eight inches tall and is bowling from the Nursery End.

But Lloyd has a problem. He has four fast bowlers and sixty overs to fill, which means twelve of them must come from someone else. He throws the ball to Larry Gomes, who bowls gentle off-spin.

**Voices**

- **Sandeep Patil** (your partner, mid-pitch): "Gomes is the one. Twelve overs of him. That is where our runs are, Kaps."
- **Gavaskar** (in your head, from the dressing room this morning): "Sixty overs is a long time. Somebody has to be there at the end."

**Decision 1 — How do you bat?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Go after Gomes. Now, before the fast men come back** | `batted=attacked` · Total: **183** · Belief +5 |
| B | **Play yourself in. Bat through to the sixtieth over** | `batted=anchored` · Total: **196** · Belief −5 · Their swagger +5 |

**Consequence text**

- **A:** Three fours in eight balls. The fourth one you hit high towards long-on, and Michael Holding does not drop those. Fifteen. You walk back past the members.

  The tail fights. Madan Lal, Kirmani and Sandhu add runs nobody expected. Marshall hits Sandhu on the helmet, and Sandhu bats on. All out in the fifty-fifth over. **One hundred and eighty-three.**

- **B:** You leave Gomes alone and the fast bowlers return, fresh. You are still there in the fifty-eighth over when Garner finds your glove. The total is a little higher. The dressing room is a little quieter: they watched their captain, the man who made 175 a week ago, block a part-time spinner.

*Design note: the anchored path gives more runs and less belief. The game does not say which is better. Neither did the scoreboard.*

### Scene 2 — The Break

**Time:** between innings · **Place:** the visitors' dressing room · **Runs in hand:** 183 *[196]*

The room smells of liniment and wet pads. Somebody has left a plate of sandwiches untouched. Through the wall you can hear the other dressing room, and it is loud.

One hundred and eighty-three. *[One hundred and ninety-six.]* In sixty overs. Against Greenidge, Haynes, Richards and Lloyd.

Nobody is looking at you, which is how you know that everybody is waiting.

**Voices**

- **Man Singh:** "Don't make a speech, Kapil. They are grown men and they can count."
- **Amarnath:** "They have to make them. Runs on the board are runs on the board. Say that."
- **Gavaskar:** "If we bowl tight and they get them in forty overs, there is no shame. If we spray it and they get them in twenty-five, we will hear about it for ten years."

**Decision 2 — What do you say?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **"It is not a winning total. But it is a fighting total. Let us make them earn every run."** | `talk=fight` · Belief +20 |
| B | **"We batted like fools. I batted like a fool. Now go out and fix it."** | `talk=anger` · Belief +5 · two bowlers start "tight": Their swagger −5, but first dropped catch risk doubles |
| C | **Say nothing. Pick up the ball, and walk to the door** | `talk=silence` · Belief +10 · Their swagger +0 |

**Consequence text**

- **A:** It is not much of a speech. Your English is still something the newspapers make jokes about. But Srikkanth laughs, and Kirmani bangs his gloves together, and when they go down the steps they are walking a little faster than they came up.
- **B:** Nobody argues. Nobody can. They go down the steps in silence, each man alone with the shot he played.
- **C:** You stand at the door until they notice. One by one, they get up. Jimmy Amarnath is the last, and he nods as he passes.

### Scene 3 — The New Ball

**Time:** West Indies' innings, over 1 · **Place:** the middle

Gordon Greenidge and Desmond Haynes walk out together. They have opened for six years. They do not hurry.

You will bowl from the Pavilion End. The question is who shares the new ball.

**Voices**

- **Kirmani:** "Ballu swings it in, and from the Nursery End the slope takes it further. Greenidge likes to leave anything outside off stump. He leaves a lot."
- **Gavaskar:** "Roger has eighteen wickets in this tournament. More than anyone, on either side. **[verify]** You give your best bowler the new ball. That is not complicated."
- **Madan Lal:** "Give it to me and I will tell you who is complicated."

**Decision 3 — Who takes the new ball with you?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Balwinder Sandhu. Swing, and the slope** | `newball=sandhu` · Wickets +1 · Runs in hand −5 · Belief +10 · Their swagger −5 |
| B | **Roger Binny. The tournament's leading wicket-taker** | `newball=binny` · Runs in hand −12 · Their swagger +0 · Binny has fewer overs left for Scene 5 |
| C | **Madan Lal. He wants it most** | `newball=madan` · Runs in hand −18 · Their swagger +5 · Madan Lal is tired by Scene 4 |

**Consequence text**

- **A:** Sandhu runs in, a turbaned medium-pacer with a bruise coming up under his helmet line. The ball starts well outside off stump. Greenidge lifts his bat high out of the way, the way he has ten thousand times.

  It comes back down the slope and hits the top of off stump.

  Greenidge stands there for a moment, bat still raised. **West Indies 5 for 1.** At slip, Gavaskar says something that is not percentages.

- **B:** Binny is accurate, as he always is. The openers watch him carefully and take what he gives. No wickets. No damage. Twelve runs gone, and they have had a look at your best bowler.
- **C:** Madan Lal bowls as if he is angry with the ball. Haynes hits him through the covers twice. He walks back to his mark faster each time.

### Scene 4 — Vivian Richards

**Time:** over 12 or so · **Place:** the middle · **Score:** West Indies 50 for 1 *(on the real path)*

He comes out without a helmet. He never wears one. He is chewing gum, and he looks at the field you have set the way a man looks at a menu.

In half an hour he has thirty-three, with seven fours. Three of them came in one over from Madan Lal. The ground has relaxed. In the stands, people who came to see West Indies win are settling in to watch how.

At this rate it will be over before tea.

Madan Lal is standing at your elbow with his hand out.

**Voices**

- **Madan Lal:** "One more over. He hit me, fine. I got him in Berbice and I will get him here. **[verify]** Give me the ball, Kaps."
- **Gavaskar:** "He has just taken twelve off him. You cannot feed a man like that. Take Madan off."
- **Kirmani:** "He is not respecting anyone. He is hitting good balls. A man who hits good balls will hit one in the air."

**Decision 4a — Do you give Madan Lal the ball?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Give him one more over** | `madan=trusted` · go to 4b |
| B | **Take him off. Bowl yourself** | `madan=removed` · Runs in hand −30 · Wickets +0 · Belief −10 · Their swagger +5 |
| C | **Slow it down. Bring on Kirti Azad's off-spin** | `madan=removed` · Runs in hand −24 · Wickets +0 · Their swagger +0 |

**Consequence text for B and C**

- **B:** You bowl fast and straight. He hits you through mid-wicket anyway. He is seeing it like a football. Haynes falls at the other end, but Richards goes on to sixty before anyone moves him.
- **C:** Azad bowls flat and Richards, for once, just milks it. The scoring slows. So does the match. He is still there.

**Decision 4b — (only after A) The ball is in the air**

Madan Lal drops it short. Richards swivels and pulls, as he has all afternoon, but this one is a fraction quicker and it takes the top edge.

It goes up. A long way up, over mid-wicket, swirling towards the Mound Stand boundary.

You are at mid-wicket. The ball is behind you, over your shoulder, twenty yards away and getting further. Yashpal Sharma is running in from the deep.

There is no time to think about any of this.

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Shout "Mine!" and run** | `catch=called` · Wickets +2 · Runs in hand −7 · Belief +30 · Their swagger −35 |
| B | **Leave it for Yashpal, who is running towards the ball** | `catch=left` · Wickets +1 · Runs in hand −20 · Belief −10 · Their swagger −5 |

**Consequence text**

- **A:** You turn your back on the pitch and run. You do not look at the ground. You do not look at Yashpal. You look at a red ball against a white sky, over your left shoulder, and it is coming down slowly, and you are still running when it falls into your hands.

  You do not fall. You do not celebrate, at first. You just keep running, because you were already running.

  Behind you, the Indians in the crowd are making a noise that Lord's has not heard before.

  **Richards, caught Kapil Dev, bowled Madan Lal, 33.** Haynes has already gone. **West Indies 57 for 3.**

- **B:** It is the percentage call. Yashpal is facing the ball and you are not. But he has thirty yards to make, and the ball dies in the breeze, and he gets one hand to it at full stretch.

  Richards does not offer another chance for an hour.

*Design note: 4b is the episode's "I have already jumped". It is a single instinct with no advisors and no time. In the interface, the option buttons appear with the ball already in the air and a three-second ring closing around them. If the ring closes, the game picks B.*

### Scene 5 — The Captain with One Leg

**Time:** over 20 or so · **Place:** the middle · **Score:** West Indies 66 for 4 *(on the real path)*

Larry Gomes has gone, caught at slip. And now Clive Lloyd is at the crease, and something is wrong.

He has pulled a muscle. He has called for a runner. The most feared captain in cricket is standing at the crease on one good leg, and he cannot get to the pitch of the ball.

He can still hit it, though. Standing still, he hits it very hard.

**Voices**

- **Amarnath:** "He cannot move his feet. Make him drive. Pitch it up outside off and put men in front of the bat."
- **Gavaskar:** "A hundred and seventeen to get, and all the time in the world. Spread the field. Make them take singles on one leg. Let the pressure do it."
- **Kirmani:** "He is angry. He wants to finish this in boundaries, to show the leg does not matter."

**Decision 5 — How do you bowl to Lloyd?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Attack. Binny, pitched up, with a ring of catchers in front of the bat** | `lloyd=attacked` · Wickets +2 · Runs in hand −10 · Belief +10 · Their swagger −20 |
| B | **Defend. Spread the field and dry up the runs** | `lloyd=defended` · Wickets +1 · Runs in hand −22 · Their swagger −5 |
| C | **Bounce him. He cannot duck on that leg** | Wickets +0 · Runs in hand −28 · Their swagger +10 · Belief −5 |

*If `newball=binny`, option A costs 6 more runs: Binny is short of overs and you must finish the spell with someone else.*

**Consequence text**

- **A:** Binny floats one up, wide of off stump. Lloyd cannot get his front foot to it, so he throws his hands. It goes flat and hard to mid-off, where you are standing, and this one you do not have to run for.

  **Lloyd, caught Kapil Dev, bowled Binny, 8.** Then Bacchus edges Sandhu behind. **West Indies 76 for 6.**

  The noise from the other dressing room has stopped.

- **B:** Singles are hard on one leg. But Lloyd waits, and waits, and when the bad ball comes he does not need to run. He makes thirty before he holes out. The runs you saved, he took back in fours.
- **C:** You are twenty-four and you have a fast bowler's pride. He hooks you into the Tavern off the back foot, twice, standing perfectly still.

### Scene 6 — The Long Afternoon

**Time:** over 30 to 42 · **Place:** the middle · **Score:** West Indies 76 for 6, and climbing

Now comes the hard part, and nobody warned you about it.

Jeff Dujon and Malcolm Marshall are not Richards and Lloyd. They do not need to be. They need 108 runs and they have thirty overs to get them. They block. They nudge. They run. Ten runs. Twenty. Forty.

The crowd, which was roaring, has gone quiet in a different way. Your fielders are starting to look at the scoreboard between balls. You have used most of your own overs, and Sandhu's, and Madan Lal's.

**119 for 6.** They need sixty-five. *[Adjust to path.]*

**Voices**

- **Gavaskar:** "You have overs left. You are the best bowler in this side. Bring yourself back and finish it."
- **Kirmani:** "They are set against pace now. They are waiting for it. Everything is coming on to the bat nicely."
- **Amarnath:** "Give me a few. I bowl so slowly they will have to make all the pace themselves. Somebody will get bored."

**Decision 6 — Who breaks the partnership?**

| | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Mohinder Amarnath. Slow, wobbling, harmless-looking** | `partnership=amarnath` · Wickets +2 · Runs in hand −5 · Belief +15 · Their swagger −15 |
| B | **Yourself. Pace, and the captain's responsibility** | `partnership=pace` · Wickets +1 · Runs in hand −18 · your overs are now finished |
| C | **Kirti Azad. Spin from both ends, and make them hit out** | `partnership=spin` · Wickets +1 · Runs in hand −26 · Their swagger +5 |

**Consequence text**

- **A:** Jimmy Amarnath runs in off a few lazy paces, as if he is bringing the ball back to someone who lost it. Dujon, who has batted for an hour without a mistake, watches one all the way on to his bat, and from his bat on to his stumps.

  He stays bent over for a long moment. Then Marshall reaches for a wide one and Gavaskar holds it at slip.

  **124 for 8.** Three for twelve, Amarnath will finish with, from the gentlest bowling in the match.

- **B:** You get Marshall, caught behind off a good one. But Dujon has been waiting for pace all afternoon, and he takes fourteen from your last two overs. You are bowled out. The ball goes to someone else now, whatever happens.
- **C:** Azad ties them down for three overs. In the fourth Marshall puts him into the Grandstand. One wicket falls, to a slog. The runs have gone.

### Scene 7 — The Last Pair

**Time:** over 50 or so · **Place:** the middle

*Shown if neither side has yet won.*

You trap Andy Roberts in front. **Nine down.**

Joel Garner and Michael Holding are the last two. They are not batsmen. But they are very tall, and they have nothing to lose, and they swing.

Around the boundary, the crowd is no longer in the seats. They are standing on the grass, ten deep, behind the rope, held back by policemen who keep looking over their shoulders. When this ends, however it ends, they are going to run.

## 5. Endings

### Ending A — Champions of the World
**Trigger:** all ten wickets taken with 30 or more runs in hand.

> They carry you. You do not remember going up the steps.
>
> On the balcony, somebody puts the Prudential Cup in your hands. It is smaller than you expected, and heavier. Below you the whole of the Lord's outfield has disappeared under people, and they are all looking up, and a great many of them are crying.
>
> At home it is past midnight. Nobody is asleep. In ten thousand towns, people who had gathered round a single television in a shop window are in the street.
>
> There is no champagne in your dressing room. Nobody thought to order any. So you walk across the corridor and ask Clive Lloyd, who is sitting very quietly, if you might borrow some of his. **[verify]**
>
> He says yes.

### Ending B — By the Width of a Bat
**Trigger:** all ten wickets taken with 10 to 29 runs in hand.

> It should not have been this close, and you will lie awake thinking about why it was. But the last one goes straight up, and Kirmani is under it shouting before it has reached the top.
>
> World champions. The margin will be forgotten. The date will not.

### Ending C — A Hundred and Eighty-Three Was Never Enough
**Trigger:** West Indies pass your total with wickets in hand, late.

> They get them in the fifty-sixth over, nine down, with a thick edge through an empty slip cordon.
>
> The newspapers are kind. *Gallant India. Brave little India.* They write about how far you came. They write about Tunbridge Wells. Nobody had expected anything else, and that is the part that stays with you: nobody had expected anything else.
>
> West Indies have won three World Cups in a row.

### Ending D — The Formalities
**Trigger:** Runs in hand reaches 0 before Scene 6.

> Richards finishes it with a six into the Pavilion. He does not watch it land.
>
> You shake hands. You say the right things. On the flight home, somebody has a newspaper with the odds in it: sixty-six to one. It seems about right.

## 6. "What really happened" screen

| Decision | What Kapil Dev did |
|---|---|
| With the bat | Attacked Larry Gomes: 15 from 8 balls, then caught by Holding. India were all out for 183 in 54.4 overs. |
| At the break | By most accounts, told his team it was not a winning total but a fighting one. **[verify wording]** |
| The new ball | Shared it with Balwinder Sandhu, who bowled Gordon Greenidge shouldering arms for 1. |
| Madan Lal | Gave him the over he asked for. Richards top-edged a pull. |
| The catch | Ran back some twenty yards from mid-wicket and took it over his shoulder. Richards c Kapil Dev b Madan Lal 33. |
| Lloyd | Kept attacking. Lloyd, batting with a runner, drove Binny to Kapil Dev for 8. |
| The partnership | Turned to Mohinder Amarnath, who bowled Dujon, had Marshall caught, and later trapped Holding lbw. |

**The scorecard:** India 183 (54.4 overs). West Indies 140 (52 overs). India won by 43 runs. Mohinder Amarnath, 26 runs and 3 for 12, was Man of the Match. Madan Lal took 3 for 31.

**Afterwards:** It was the first time West Indies had lost a World Cup final. Four years later the World Cup was held in India and Pakistan, the first outside England. A boy of ten named Sachin Tendulkar watched the final on television in Bombay and has said it is why he wanted to play. The writer who had promised to eat his words did, in print, with a photograph. **[verify]**

**The other view** (always shown): West Indies beat India 5–0 in a one-day series a few months later, and many of their players have said they lost the final through carelessness, not to a better side. Lloyd's injury mattered. So did a pitch that helped medium pace. India's own players have said, cheerfully, that they would have lost that match nine times out of ten. Whether a captain's choices decide a cricket match, or only decorate it, is something cricket has never settled.

## 8. Living people and likeness

Almost everyone in this episode is alive, famous, and has told this story himself many times. That raises the bar.

- **Every line is invented and kept to cricket.** No player is shown as lazy, frightened, selfish or at fault for a defeat. On losing paths the text blames the total and the opposition, never a named Indian player.
- **Counterfactuals belong to the player.** If the catch goes down, it goes down because *the player* left it, not because Yashpal Sharma dropped it. He "gets one hand to it at full stretch".
- **West Indians are written with respect.** They were the best team in the world and the script says so.
- **Likeness and names are a legal question.** Indian courts have protected sportspeople's personality rights, and the 1983 story has been licensed for a feature film. Before release, get legal advice on using real names and recognisable figures, and consider approaching the players or their representatives.
- **Use nothing from the film *83*.** It is a dramatisation with its own invented scenes, and it is somebody else's copyright.

## 9. What this episode needs built

- **A new environment:** Lord's. The dressing room and balcony for Scene 2 and the endings; the middle, with the pavilion behind, for everything else.
- **Outdoor figures in whites,** standing, not seated at a table. Fielders at a distance can be simple shapes, like the crowd in the party hall.
- **A scoreboard HUD:** score, overs, runs in hand and wickets, styled like an old manual scoreboard.
- **A timed choice** for Decision 4b: a three-second ring, with a default if it runs out.
- **Sound:** crowd murmur that rises and falls with Belief, leather on willow, and a roar.
